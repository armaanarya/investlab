## Design consequences (these overturn parts of the original spec)
W1. Wharton is NOT an end-of-day game. A next-close execution model is wrong here.
    But see W2 - it barely matters.
W2. $25/trade + 200-trade cap + "this is NOT a trading game" + anti-churn judging
    => the momentum/breakout sleeve from spec section 8.2 is ACTIVELY WRONG for
    Wharton. On $500k, 200 trades = $5,000 of commission, 1% of the portfolio.
    Wharton wants a low-turnover, thesis-driven, client-matched portfolio.
W3. IBIT is banned at Wharton on two independent grounds: explicit crypto ban, and
    ETFs must come from the Approved List.
W4. The AI policy is load-bearing for how we build. The tool must produce EVIDENCE
    (data, charts, screens, comparisons) that students evaluate and write up
    themselves. It must not draft the thesis, the IPS, or the trading notes.
    Anything it does draft must be cited APA as AI-generated.
W5. Trading notes are audited => journal must capture the student's OWN reasoning
    at trade time, with the tool supplying supporting numbers only.
W6. Starting capital is unknown until Sep 15 and was $500k last year. Do not
    hard-code $100k anywhere. Capital is a config value.
