# investlab design spec

Date: 2026-09-06
Status: approved by user for implementation

## 1. Purpose

A local Python decision-support tool for two high school investing competitions
that run concurrently from September to December 2026:

- **DECA Stock Market Game** (SIFMA platform), Sept 8 to Dec 4 2026
- **Wharton Global High School Investment Competition** (WInS / StockTrak),
  trading Sept 28 to Dec 4 2026

The tool produces a daily order sheet the student types into each platform by
hand, tracks the resulting portfolio, enforces competition rules, and exports
evidence for the graded deliverables.

## 2. Non-goals

Stated once, here, so they are not re-litigated in any plan or task.

- **No platform integration.** Neither competition exposes an API. All orders
  are entered manually. The tool never authenticates to, scrapes, or posts to
  either platform.
- **No real money.** No broker integration of any kind.
- **No historical backtest of mutual funds or bonds.** Free data does not exist
  for the SMG bond universe or reliable point-in-time fund NAVs. The compliance
  sleeve is *accounted for*, never backtested. Any report touching it says so.
- **No spike-prediction ML in v1.** Deferred. See section 11.
- **No survivorship-free universe.** All backtests are fixed-universe and
  carry a permanent selection-bias label.
- **No Backtrader, no VectorBT.** See section 6.
- **No web UI in v1.** CLI only.

## 3. Binding compliance constraint: AI authorship

Wharton's rules state: "You may not submit any work generated by an AI program
as your own," and any AI-generated material included in a report must be cited
in APA style. DECA requires that portfolios "reflect the individual team's
investment strategy."

This is a design constraint, not a disclaimer:

- The tool emits **evidence**: screens, rankings, prices, costs, risk numbers,
  constraint checks, charts, reconciliation tables.
- The tool never drafts a thesis, an Investment Policy Statement, a trading
  note, or any prose that would be submitted as student work.
- The journal command **prompts the student for their own reasoning** and
  attaches supporting numbers to it. It does not suggest reasoning.
- Every generated artifact carries a provenance footer naming the tool, the
  version, and the data source, so it can be cited if used.

Any task that would produce submittable prose is out of scope. If a plan step
appears to require it, the implementer stops and flags it.

## 4. Verified competition facts

Full detail lives in `docs/rules/deca-verified.md` and
`docs/rules/wharton-verified.md`. These are the load-bearing differences:

| | DECA SMG | Wharton WInS |
|---|---|---|
| Execution | **End of day.** Order placed 9:30a-4:00p ET fills at *that day's* close. After hours fills next business day's close. | **Real time** during market hours. After-hours fills at next open. Bonds/international EOD. |
| Limit orders | Do not rest. One pricing attempt. | Standard |
| Starting cash | $100,000 | **Unknown for 2026-27.** Was $500,000 in 2025-26. Config value, never hard-coded. |
| Commission | $5/trade + SEC fee on sells | $25/stock trade, $10/treasury |
| Trade cap | none | **200 trades total** |
| Margin | up to 50% of equity, 7%/yr | banned |
| Shorting | allowed (not toward diversification) | banned |
| Universe | NYSE + NASDAQ only, >= $3, >= $25M cap, >= 10 shares/buy | any platform exchange, >= $5, foreign + ADR ok |
| ETFs | allowed, **classified as stocks** | **approved list only**, >= 1 required |
| Crypto / commodities | banned (bitcoin and commodities named) | banned |
| Diversification | **hard**: $10k net cost each in stocks/funds/bonds by **Oct 23 2026 4pm ET**, held to Dec 4 | **soft**: >= 1 approved ETF; rest judged qualitatively |
| Scoring | % return vs S&P 500 growth, top 25 per region | strategy + articulation, **explicitly not returns** |

**IBIT and all spot-crypto and physically-backed commodity trusts (GLD, SLV)
are prohibited at both.** The tool hard-blocks them and explains why.

## 5. Strategy posture: two different games

The single most important design decision. These competitions reward opposite
behavior, so they get separate strategies, not one strategy with a config flag.

### DECA: tournament-optimized, deliberately concentrated

Top 25 per region advance. That is a step-function payoff, so the objective is
`P(rank >= qualifying)`, not `E[return]`. Above a threshold, variance helps.

- Target 5 to 8 long equity positions, not 15.
- Position ceiling 30% of equity at purchase (the verified 20% x 1.5 rule).
- Compliance minimums parked first: >= $10k net cost each in a mutual fund and
  a bond, budgeted at $10,100 to absorb the $5 fee.
- **No margin at start.** Margin is a late-game variance lever only, enabled by
  explicit config after November 1, never by default. Rationale: 7% annual
  interest, and a maintenance breach triggers SMG auto-liquidation after seven
  days.
- **No shorting in v1.** Permitted by the rules, but shorts do not count toward
  diversification and add a failure mode the ledger would have to model.

### Wharton: low turnover, mandate-matched

Judged on strategy and articulation. $25/trade and a 200-trade cap.

- Target 8 to 15 positions, held for weeks to months.
- Hard budget: **<= 40 total trades** for the season, leaving headroom against
  the 200 cap and keeping commission drag under ~0.2% of a $500k book.
- No momentum sleeve. Candidate generation is a screen the student reviews,
  not a signal that fires.
- Every position must map to a stated client objective once the case study
  lands Sept 15. Until then the profile is `WHARTON_UNVERIFIED` and the tool
  refuses to emit an order sheet, emitting a research screen instead.

## 6. Architecture

One package, `src/investlab/`. A shared core, then two competition modules that
do not import each other.

```
src/investlab/
  config.py          typed config, profile loading
  money.py           Decimal money, share rounding, cost model
  calendar.py        NYSE sessions, holidays, ET timestamps
  data/
    cache.py         on-disk parquet cache, the reproducibility boundary
    providers.py     Provider protocol + yfinance and stooq adapters
    universe.py      fixed ticker list, declared up front, bias-labeled
  features/
    indicators.py    EMA, MACD, RSI, ATR, realized vol, breakout, rel volume
    ranking.py       cross-sectional percentile ranks
  portfolio/
    ledger.py        positions, lots, cash, fills, corporate actions
    sizing.py        integer share solver under constraints
    risk.py          exposure, concentration, drawdown state
  competitions/
    base.py          Profile protocol: rules, costs, execution, eligibility
    deca.py          DECA rules engine + EOD execution + diversification clock
    wharton.py       Wharton rules engine + trade budget + mandate mapping
  backtest/
    engine.py        purpose-built daily event loop
    baselines.py     cash, buy-and-hold, equal-weight, simple momentum
  reports/
    tearsheet.py     HTML + CSV export with provenance footer
    charts.py        matplotlib PNG exports for the deck and report
  journal.py         student-authored decision log
  cli.py             typer commands
```

**Why no Backtrader.** Two reasons, one design and one fatal.

The design reason: the original spec pins Backtrader as authoritative then
spends a full section describing how its defaults are wrong here: close-only
fills, no intraday stops, game-specific commissions, frozen quantities, and a
custom broker adapter. At that point the adapter is the engine. For daily bars,
long-only, at most 15 positions, a purpose-built loop of roughly 400 lines is
smaller, fully testable, and correct by construction.

The fatal reason: **Backtrader does not run on Python 3.12.** Last commit
2023-04-19. `backtrader/lineiterator.py` lines 229 and 237 call
`collections.Iterable`, removed in Python 3.10, and `btrun.py` imports `imp`,
removed in 3.12. Other modules carry a `collections.abc` compat shim;
`lineiterator.py` does not. It imports on 3.9 and raises `AttributeError` at
runtime on 3.12.

VectorBT 1.1.0 is healthy and supports 3.11 to 3.14, but it is array-based
machinery for sweeping thousands of parameter combinations, which this project
does not do. If a third-party engine is ever wanted, `bt` (MIT, built for
long-only daily rebalancing) is the right shape. Not needed for v1.

**The cache is the reproducibility boundary.** Every run reads from the parquet
cache, never the network. `investlab data pull` is the only command that
touches the internet. A run manifest records the cache hash, so a backtest is
reproducible offline and the tool keeps working when Yahoo breaks.

**Data providers.** Verified live 2026-09-06:

- **yfinance 1.7.0 primary.** Healthy and actively maintained, contrary to its
  reputation. Measured: 502 tickers x 5 years in 52 seconds, 493/502 first-try.
  Covers stocks, ETFs, **mutual funds** (VFIAX, FXAIX and friends all return
  full adjusted history), bond ETFs, and yield indices in one library, no key.
  Two required workarounds: convert `.` to `-` in tickers (BRK.B -> BRK-B), and
  retry transient failures once.
- **Tiingo free tier fallback.** 500 unique symbols/month, 1000 req/day, 30+
  years of history, mutual fund NAVs. Set it up before yfinance breaks, not
  after.
- **Stooq is dead for programmatic use.** Since ~April 2026 it requires an API
  key *and* solves a JavaScript proof-of-work challenge; the CSV endpoint
  returns "Access denied" even with the PoW cookie. Do not design around it.
- **pandas-datareader is not an option.** Version 0.11.0 (June 2026) deleted
  every equity reader including Yahoo and Stooq. It is macro-data only now.
  Any tutorial using `pdr.get_data_yahoo` is dead code.

Bonds: individual corporate bond price history is unobtainable at zero budget
(FINRA Enhanced TRACE is $2,000/yr for 18-month-delayed data). Use bond ETFs
plus the Treasury par yield curve CSV and FRED `fredgraph.csv?id=DGS10`, both
free and keyless. State the limitation in every report that touches bonds.

A provider outage disables that provider, not the tool.

## 7. Ledger invariants

Enforced by property tests, not by convention.

- `equity == cash + marked_positions + receivables - liabilities`
- Every fill references an order; every order references a decision
- Share quantities are integers and are **never rounded up**
- Money is `Decimal`, quantized to cents at every boundary
- A split preserves economic value; a dividend is credited exactly once
- Cash cannot go negative in a profile where margin is disabled
- Two candidates cannot spend the same dollar: cash is reserved sequentially
  in deterministic candidate order

## 8. Sizing

`L(q) = q*(P-S) + entry_fee + exit_fee` where P is the conservative entry bound
and S the protective reference. Choose the largest integer q satisfying:

1. `L(q) <= risk_fraction * equity`
2. `q*P + entry_fee <= spendable_cash`
3. post-trade position weight <= profile position ceiling
4. profile minimum lot (DECA: 10 shares on buys)
5. price eligibility (DECA: >= $3; Wharton: >= $5)

If the minimum permitted quantity violates any cap, emit `NO_TRADE` with the
binding reason. Never force the minimum. Planned risk is reported as *planned*,
with a gap-stress figure beside it showing the loss at a 20% adverse gap.

## 9. Daily operating loop

The command the student actually runs, `investlab daily --profile deca`:

1. Pull the day's bars into the cache (or fail loudly and use yesterday's,
   labeled stale)
2. Reconcile the ledger against the pasted or imported platform statement
3. Evaluate exits on held positions
4. Screen and rank candidates
5. Size orders under all constraints
6. Check profile rules: diversification clock, position ceilings, trade budget
7. Print the order sheet: ticker, action, integer shares, estimated cost,
   the reason, and the constraint that bound it
8. Prompt for journal entries on anything actioned

Exit codes: 0 success, 2 invalid config, 3 data unavailable, 4 unresolved
competition rule, 5 verification failure. Never 0 after swallowing a failure.

## 10. Testing

Hand-computable fixtures, property-based invariants, and a leakage test that is
non-negotiable: **prefix invariance.** Append future bars to a dataset, recompute,
and assert every earlier feature, signal, order and fill is byte-identical. A
system that fails this is producing fiction.

Plus: DECA execution timing (an order placed during hours fills at *that* day's
close), diversification net-cost math including the $5 fee, the one-business-day
replacement clock, the appreciation exception (hold, don't add), Wharton trade
budget enforcement, IBIT and GLD rejection, integer share rounding, and split
and dividend handling.

Target 90% branch coverage in `portfolio/`, `competitions/`, and `backtest/`.
Coverage does not substitute for the adversarial cases above.

## 11. Cut from v1, with reasons

- **Spike-prediction ML.** Rare-event classification on a few years of free
  daily bars, evaluated honestly, will almost certainly return "no robust
  edge." That is a research project, not a competition tool, and the games end
  Dec 4.
- **Point-in-time fundamentals from EDGAR.** Turning XBRL into correct filing-
  vintage quarterly data is weeks of work and the quality strategy is optional.
- **Streamlit UI.** Seven pages of dashboard for a tool run once a day by one
  person. CLI plus PNG exports covers it.
- **Intraday and order-book views.** Requires data we do not have, and DECA
  prices at the close regardless.

## 12. Open items requiring the student

1. **SMG Local Rules** page, inside the team portfolio under Resources ->
   Rules of the Game. Both primary sources say DECA-specific rules live there.
2. Whether SMG offers CSV export of holdings and transactions. Changes the
   reconciliation path from paste-parsing to file import.
3. **Sept 15**: Wharton case study, trading requirements, starting capital,
   and the Approved ETF List.
4. Advisor to email decasmg@sifma.org for written confirmation on any
   borderline security. Students get no reply directly.

## 13. Delivery phases

**Phase 1 (core, days 1-4).** money, calendar, cache, providers, universe,
indicators, ledger, sizing, journal, CLI skeleton. Ends with a hand-checkable
sizing example and a passing property suite.

**Phase 2 (competitions, parallel).** DECA module and Wharton module, built
concurrently by separate agents against the frozen core interfaces.

**Phase 3 (evidence).** Backtest engine, baselines, tear sheet, chart exports.

Phase 2 is the reason the core interfaces in section 6 are frozen before any
competition work starts: the two agents must not touch the same files.
